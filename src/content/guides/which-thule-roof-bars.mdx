Thule's UK range has four main roof bars, plus a few specialist options. Which one is right for you depends less on the bar itself than on your roof, what you plan to carry and how much you want to spend. Here's how they compare, in plain terms.

## First: the bar is only one part of the rack

A Thule roof rack has three parts, and you need all three:

- **Roof bars**: the crossbars, which is what this guide compares.
- **Foot pack**: four feet chosen for your roof type, whether that's a normal roof, raised rails, flush rails, fixpoints or gutters.
- **Fitting kit**: the car-specific part made to match your roof's dimensions. Thule says cars with raised rails don't need one.

When you choose a complete system on Thule's site, the bar width, feet and fitting kit are all matched to your car. If you're not sure what roof you have, our guide to [fitting a roof box without roof rails](/guides/roof-box-without-roof-rails/) walks through each type, and our guide to [Thule fitting kits](/guides/thule-fitting-kits-explained/) explains the kit numbers.

## The four bars compared

| | WingBar Evo | WingBar Edge | ProBar Evo | SquareBar Evo |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Profile | Aerodynamic wing, aluminium | Same wing profile, sits low | Wide aluminium tri-slot | Square galvanised steel, black polymer coat |
| Noise | Very quiet (Thule's WindDiffuser) | Very quiet, lowest profile | No low-noise claim from Thule | Expect more wind noise than a wing bar |
| Max load (Thule rating) | 100 kg | 75 kg | 100 kg | 100 kg |
| Fit | Through-bar: ends extend past the feet | Flush: bar finishes at the feet | Through-bar | Through-bar |
| T-track | Yes, upper T-track | Yes | Yes, upper and rear | No |
| Colours (UK) | Aluminium or black | Aluminium or black | Aluminium | Black |
| Price (relative) | Mid | Highest | Mid to high | Lowest |
| Best for | Most cars and most uses | A factory look on normal roofs, flush rails and fixpoints | Heavy, long or wide loads | Tight budgets and occasional use |

A few notes on reading the table:

- **Max load** is Thule's rating for the bars (for WingBar Edge, the complete system). Your real limit is the lowest of the bars, the feet and the car's maximum roof load in the handbook. For example, Thule's Evo Clamp and Evo Flush Rail feet are rated to 75 kg.
- **Through-bar vs flush**: a through-bar extends beyond the feet, giving you more bar to mount things on. A flush bar stops at the feet for a neater, built-in look.
- **Price** is based on Thule UK's starting prices for complete systems. What you pay depends on the feet and kit your car needs.

## Thule WingBar Evo

This is the bar we'd point most people towards first. It's an aluminium wing-shaped bar, and Thule describes it as giving "an exceptionally quiet ride" thanks to its WindDiffuser technology, which disturbs the airflow to reduce noise and drag.

It's rated to 100 kg, and the upper T-track with Thule's QuickAccess interface lets you slide roof box, bike and ski carrier fittings in and out cleanly. Because it's a through-bar, there's more usable width, which helps if you want a roof box and a bike carrier side by side.

It fits all the main roof types through the Evo foot range, and comes in aluminium or black.

## Thule WingBar Edge

WingBar Edge uses the same aerodynamic bar profile, but the bar and foot form one low, integrated unit that finishes flush with the feet. Thule pitches it as its premium low-profile system, with the look of a factory-fitted rack.

The trade-offs:

- It's rated to 75 kg as a system, lower than the 100 kg bar rating of WingBar Evo.
- Because the bar stops at the feet, there's less length to work with, so check wide accessories will fit.
- Thule warns that it sits so low that it may not be possible to open a sunroof with the racks fitted.
- It uses its own Edge foot packs (Edge Clamp, Edge Flush Rail and Edge Fixpoint in the UK range), not the Evo feet.

It's usually the most expensive of the four, and comes in aluminium or black.

## Thule ProBar Evo

ProBar Evo is the workhorse. It's a wide aluminium bar with what Thule calls a tri-slot design: upper and rear T-tracks for accessories, plus a lower T-track that attaches it to the feet on the roof. That means you can run several accessories at once, independently or in combination.

The bars are rated to 100 kg and come in lengths up to 220 cm, which suits vans and wider loads. Thule makes no particular claims about noise for ProBar, so if quietness is your priority, WingBar Evo is the better pick. In Thule's UK range it comes in aluminium.

## Thule SquareBar Evo

SquareBar Evo is Thule's classic square bar in its current form: galvanised steel with a black polymer coating that Thule says adds grip for cargo. The bars are rated to 100 kg, which matches the aluminium bars, but there's no T-track, so accessories clamp around the bar instead of sliding into a slot.

Most Thule roof boxes and carriers can still fit square bars, so it's a sound choice if you only use a roof box once or twice a year and want to keep costs down. Expect more wind noise than a wing-shaped bar, especially at motorway speeds.

## Which should you choose?

- **A roof box and bikes on a family car**: WingBar Evo.
- **Flush rails or fixpoints, and you want the cleanest look**: WingBar Edge, as long as your accessories fit and the sunroof clears.
- **Ladders, long loads, a van or several accessories at once**: ProBar Evo.
- **Occasional use on a budget**: SquareBar Evo, or look at [used and ex-display Thule bars](/used-thule/). As Peter Mellody said in his Google review: "I got a second hand like new set of roof bars and a roof box for almost half the price of buying new."
