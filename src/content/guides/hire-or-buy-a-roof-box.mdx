If you only need a roof box for one holiday a year, hiring is usually cheaper and easier. If you'll use it for several trips a year, buying starts to pay off, and buying a used Thule box gets you there faster.

Here's how the costs compare, and the questions worth asking before you decide.

## Three questions to ask yourself

1. **How many trips will you take in the next two or three years?** If it's one or two, hire. If it's five or more, it's worth pricing up a used box.
2. **Where will it live?** If there's no garage, shed or loft space for a family-size box (an XL is 215 cm long), hiring saves the hassle.
3. **Do you know what size you need?** If not, hire first and buy once you know what works for your car and your family.

## What hiring costs

We don't list our hire prices here because they change with the season and the box. See our [roof box hire page](/roof-box-hire/) for current prices.

For a sense of the wider market, published prices from a sample of UK hire firms at the time of writing (October 2026) ran from about £45 to £125 for roughly a week. Where a hire lands in that range depends on:

- **Box size.** Bigger boxes cost more to hire.
- **Season.** Some firms charge more from spring to the end of summer.
- **What's included.** Some prices include roof bars and fitting; others charge for bars separately.

## What buying costs

At the time of writing, Thule's UK list prices for its current roof boxes run from £459.99 (Pulse 2 M) to £899.99 (Motion 3 XXL). If your car doesn't already have roof bars, add a set of bars and the right fitting kit.

Buying used brings that down a long way. As one of our customers, Peter Mellody, put it in his Google review:

If you take two or three week-long trips a year, a new box covers its cost in roughly two to six years. A used box gets there much sooner, and either way you can sell it when you're done, which this maths doesn't count.

## When hiring makes sense

- **You need a box once a year or less.** One summer holiday or one ski trip rarely justifies buying.
- **You've nowhere to keep it.** An XL box is around 215 cm long and weighs 20 to 23 kg, so it needs a garage wall or a good chunk of loft.
- **You're not sure what size you need.** Hiring lets you try a size on a real trip before you spend money on one.
- **Your car has no roof bars.** You can hire [roof bars](/roof-bar-hire/) for the trip rather than buying a set.

## When buying used makes sense

If you're away several times a year (summer holidays, half-terms, camping weekends, ski trips), owning a box starts to make sense, and a used one is the cheapest way in.

- **It's ready when you are.** No booking, no collecting, no working round other people's holiday dates.
- **Thule boxes are built to last.** Older Motion XT, Force XT and Touring boxes are still common on the used market, and a well-looked-after one does the same job for much less than a new box.
- **Parts are still available.** We supply keys and lock cylinders (N001 to N250) and repair locks and hinges, including parts for discontinued boxes, through our [Thule spare parts](/thule-spare-parts/) service.
- **You can sell it on.** Thule is a well-known name and parts are easy to get, so a complete box in good condition is easier to sell when your family outgrows it.

## When buying new makes sense

- You want a specific current model, colour or size, such as a Low box to fit under a garage door.
- You'll use it heavily for years and want it from day one.
- You'd rather know exactly how the box has been used.

## Costs people forget

- **Roof bars and fitting kit.** A box needs bars. If you're not sure what fits, use our [which roof bars fit my car](/which-roof-bars-fit-my-car/) form.
- **Storage.** Owning a box means finding somewhere dry to keep it between trips, and a family-size box is often around 2 metres long.
- **Fuel.** A roof box adds drag, so it's worth taking it off between trips rather than leaving it on all year.
- **Time.** If you own a box, you'll be fitting and removing it yourself.
