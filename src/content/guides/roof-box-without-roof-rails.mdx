It's one of the questions we hear most in the workshop, usually from someone who has just noticed their car has a smooth roof. Rails aren't what makes a roof box possible. What matters is the right pair of roof bars, held on by feet and a fitting kit that suit your particular roof.

## You always need roof bars

A roof box never sits directly on the roof, and it doesn't clamp straight onto roof rails either. Thule's safety instructions say roof boxes must only be mounted on approved rack systems. The box's own clamps (PowerClick on the current Motion 3 and Force 3) grip the two bars, and the bars carry the load down to the car.

A Thule roof rack is made of three parts:

- **Roof bars**: the two crossbars the box clamps to, such as WingBar Evo or SquareBar Evo.
- **Foot pack**: four feet, chosen to suit your type of roof.
- **Fitting kit**: the car-specific part, made to match the shape and dimensions of your roof. Thule says cars with raised rails don't need one.

Get all three right and the box goes on securely. Get one wrong and nothing fits, which is why the fitting kit number matters so much. Our guide to [Thule fitting kits](/guides/thule-fitting-kits-explained/) explains how the kits and numbers work.

## The five roof types and what fits each

Before anything else, work out which roof you have. Thule groups cars into five main types, and each one takes a different foot.

### Normal roof (no rails)

A smooth roof with no rails, no covered mounting points and no gutters. Thule calls this a "normal roof". The Thule Evo Clamp foot is made for these cars, and it needs a vehicle-specific fitting kit. The kit's rubber pads and brackets are shaped to your car's door openings, so the foot clamps around the edge of the roof when the doors are open, then the doors close over it.

### Raised rails

Rails that run front to back with a visible gap underneath, big enough to get your fingers round. These are the simplest to fit: Thule's Evo Raised Rail feet clamp straight onto the rails and don't need a fitting kit.

### Flush rails

Rails that sit tight against the roof with no gap underneath. They look like rails, but a raised-rail foot can't grip them. You need Evo Flush Rail feet plus a car-specific fitting kit.

### Fixpoints

Factory-fitted mounting points, usually hidden under small flaps or a trim strip along the roof edge. Evo Fixpoint feet bolt into these points using a car-specific fitting kit.

### Rain gutters

A gutter channel running along each side of the roof, mostly found on older cars and some vans. Thule makes gutter feet, such as the 951 and the taller 953, that clamp to the gutter itself.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Normal roof | Smooth roof, no rails or covers | Evo Clamp | Yes, car-specific | 75 kg |

## How clamp feet work on a bare roof

This is the setup most people mean when they ask about fitting a box without rails, so it's worth explaining properly.

The fitting kit is the clever bit. It contains rubber pads and brackets shaped for your exact car, and the Evo Clamp foot pulls the bracket tight against the edge of the roof inside the door opening. Thule supplies a torque limiter key that clicks when the foot is tight enough, so you aren't guessing.

The fitting instructions for your kit give the exact positions for the feet. That matters for a roof box, because the box needs the bars a certain distance apart. Thule's roof box safety instructions say that, where no special fitting positions are given, you should keep at least 700 mm (about 27.5 in) between the bars. The box manual tells you the bar spread it needs.

## Roof load: the number that matters most

Whatever roof you have, there's a weight limit, and it's usually lower than people expect. Thule's rule is that the lowest limit in the chain applies:

- the maximum roof load in your car's handbook
- the rating of the feet (75 kg for Evo Clamp and Evo Flush Rail)
- the rating of the bars

And everything on the roof counts. Thule's calculation is: weight of the roof rack, plus weight of the roof box, plus weight of what's inside. A Thule Force 3 L weighs 18.7 kg empty and a Motion 3 L weighs 20 kg, so on a car with a 75 kg roof limit, a quarter of your allowance is used before you've packed a thing, and the bars come off that too.

Don't improvise with a kit meant for a similar model. If your car doesn't appear in Thule's fit guide, there may be no approved fitting for it. Ask us before buying anything.
</Callout>
