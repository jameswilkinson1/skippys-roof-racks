Fewer than the litres suggest, because suitcases are rigid boxes and roof boxes are long, shallow and tapered. A medium 400 litre box usually takes three or four cabin cases, and you need an XL or bigger to carry two large cases comfortably.

Below you'll find a table by box size, the reasons the numbers vary, and the weight limits that often matter more than the space.

## The suitcase sizes we're working with

Suitcase sizes vary by brand, so the table below uses typical dimensions. Measure your own cases, including wheels and handles, before you rely on any of these numbers.

| Case | Typical size | Approx. volume |
|---|---|---|
| Cabin | 55 x 40 x 20 cm | About 44 litres |
| Medium | 65–70 cm tall, 25–30 cm deep | About 60–80 litres |
| Large | 75–80 cm tall, 30 cm or more deep | About 90–120 litres |

On paper, a 500 litre box could hold eleven cabin cases. In practice it's four or five, which shows how much the shape of the box matters.

## How many suitcases fit, by roof box size

These are approximate figures worked out from Thule's published internal dimensions, with the cases lying flat in a single layer. Your result will depend on the internal shape of your box and the shape of your cases.

| Box size | Example Thule boxes | Cabin cases | Medium cases | Large cases |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| About 320 L (300–330 L) | Force 3 S, Sport models, older Touring S | 2–3 | 1–2 | 0–1 |
| Around 400 L | Motion 3 M, Force 3 M, Pulse 2 M | 3–4 | 2 | 1–2 |
| Around 450 L | Motion 3 L, Force 3 L, Pulse 2 L | 4 | 2, plus a cabin case | 1–2 |
| Around 500 L | Motion 3 XL, Force 3 XL, Pulse 2 XL | 4–5 | 2–3 | 2, maybe plus a cabin case |
| 590 L and up | Motion 3 XXL, older Motion XT XXL | 5 or more | 3–4 | 2, plus 1–2 cabin cases |

In the smallest size, shape matters most. A short, wide box like the Force 3 S (131 x 78 cm inside) can take one large case, but a long, narrow Sport box (around 50 cm wide inside) usually can't.

If your luggage sits on the line between two sizes, either go up a size or plan to put one case in the boot and fill the box with soft bags. It's far easier to leave a little room spare than to fight the lid shut on your driveway at 6am.

## Why the numbers vary so much

**Internal height.** Standard Motion 3, Force 3 and Pulse 2 boxes are around 37–39 cm tall inside at the highest point. Two cabin cases stacked flat come to about 40 cm, so in most boxes cases go in one layer. Low boxes are shallower again (29–33 cm inside on the Motion 3 Low models), which suits skis and soft bags better than cases.

**Tapered ends and curved sides.** Thule quotes internal dimensions at the longest, widest and tallest usable point. Near the nose, the tail and the edges there's less room, so a case that fits on paper may not fit in the corner you'd planned.

**Width.** Most boxes are 69–77 cm wide inside, which isn't enough for two cabin cases side by side. Older, wider boxes can be different: Thule lists the Motion XT XXL at 84 cm wide inside, enough for cabin cases two abreast for part of the length.

**Similar litres, different shapes.** The older Thule Dynamic M has 320 litres but is only 27 cm tall inside, while the Force 3 S has 300 litres and 35 cm of height. On paper the Dynamic is bigger; for suitcases, the Force 3 S is better.

**Fixings inside.** The mounting knobs sit on the floor inside the box, so leave room around them.

## Weight usually runs out before space

Every current Motion 3, Force 3 and Pulse 2 box is rated to carry 75 kg, but your car's roof limit is often the tighter one. You'll find the maximum roof load in your vehicle handbook, and it includes the roof bars and the box itself.

Current Motion 3, Force 3 and Pulse 2 boxes weigh roughly 15 to 25 kg empty. A packed large suitcase can easily top 20 kg, so two of them plus a cabin bag can get close to the box's limit, and past some cars' roof limits once the box and bars are counted. The Highway Code says you must not overload your vehicle.

<Callout type="warning" title="Do the maths first">Take your car's maximum roof load from the handbook, subtract the weight of the bars and the empty box, and what's left is your luggage allowance. Weigh the cases on bathroom scales if you're not sure.</Callout>

## Hard cases or soft bags?

If you've got the choice, put the hard cases in the boot and the soft bags in the roof box. Holdalls and duffels squash into the tapered ends and fill the gaps around everything else, so you get far more use out of the space.

Soft roof bags work on the same principle. One of our customers, Ryan K, used a soft roof bag rather than a box, and summed it up in his Google review:

> "We purchased a 595L waterproof roof luggage bag which carried 4 suitcases (one 75cm and 3 carry ons)."

That's roughly in line with the table above for a box of a similar size.

## Packing suitcases into a roof box

- **Heaviest case in the middle,** between the roof bars, with lighter bags towards the ends.
- **Lay cases flat** and push them right into position before adding anything else.
- **Fill the gaps** with coats, shoes in bags and other soft items so nothing can slide.
- **Close the lid without pressure.** If you have to lean on it, something needs to come out.
- **Lock it and check it.** Make sure the lid is shut and locked along its whole length, then give the box a firm tug before you set off.
