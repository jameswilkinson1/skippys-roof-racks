Packing a roof box well makes the car handle more predictably, keeps your kit dry and makes the most of every litre. The rules are simple: think about weight first and space second.

## Start with the numbers: box limit vs roof load

There are two limits to stay under, and it's easy to check only one of them.

- **The box's maximum load.** Current Thule Motion 3, Force 3 and Pulse 2 boxes are rated at 75 kg. Some older ranges are lower, such as the Touring at 50 kg, so check the label inside the box.
- **Your car's maximum roof load.** This is in the vehicle handbook. Thule advises using the dynamic figure (the limit when the car is moving), which is lower than the static one.

The car's roof load has to cover **the bars, the box and the load together**. Say, as an example, your handbook allows 75 kg, your bars weigh 5 kg and your box 20 kg. That leaves 50 kg for luggage, not the 75 kg printed on the box. Use your own figures, as every car, bar and box is different.

The Highway Code (rule 98) says you must not overload your vehicle and you must secure your load.

<Callout type="tip" title="Steve's tip">Weigh anything that feels heavy on the bathroom scales before it goes up. A bag of tins or a full cool box adds up far faster than you'd expect.</Callout>

## Light and bulky up top, heavy in the car

Anything on the roof raises the car's centre of gravity. Thule's instructions note that a loaded box changes how the car behaves in crosswinds, cornering and braking. So the roof box is for things that take up space but don't weigh much, and the heavy stuff stays low inside the car.

| Good in the roof box | Better in the car |
|---|---|
| Sleeping bags, duvets and pillows | Heavy suitcases and holdalls |
| Clothes in soft bags | Food, drinks and cool boxes |
| Skis and snowboards (check the box's max ski length) | Tools, tins and anything dense |
| Folded pushchair or travel cot | Laptops, cameras and valuables |
| Beach and camping kit, chairs, wetsuits | Things you'll need during the journey |
| Coats and walking boots | Liquids that could leak |

For ideas on what fits in each size, see our guide to [what fits in a roof box](/guides/what-fits-in-a-roof-box/).

## Weight distribution: the quarter-half-quarter rule

Thule's own packing advice is to put about **half the weight in the middle of the box, between the roof bars**, and about a quarter at each end. In practice:

1. Put the heaviest items in the centre, over and between the bars.
2. Put lighter, squashier things in the nose and tail, where the box tapers.
3. Keep the load even from side to side, so the car doesn't lean one way.
4. Keep the heaviest items low in the box rather than stacked on top.

Weight in the nose or tail acts like a lever on the bars and the clamps, which is why the middle is the place for anything substantial.

## Use soft bags, not hard cases

Roof boxes are tapered at both ends and shallow compared with a boot, so rigid suitcases waste a lot of space. Soft holdalls and duffels squash into the corners and fill the box much better. Thule sells its GoPack duffel set as a roof box accessory, but any soft bags will do.

Packing cubes or several medium bags are easier to lift in and out than one huge bag. They're also easier to spread evenly.

## Strap the load down

Many Thule boxes come with internal straps that clip to anchor points inside the box, like the three in the photo above. Use them. Pass them over the load and pull them snug so nothing can slide forward under hard braking.

If your box has lost its straps, replacements are available from our [Thule spare parts](/thule-spare-parts/) range. Fill any gaps with soft items such as pillows or coats, so the load can't shuffle about.

## Don't overfill the lid

If you have to lean on the lid to shut it, the box is too full. Pressure on the lid can stop it latching properly, put strain on the hinges and lid lifters, and let water in along the seam. On boxes with Thule's SlideLock, the lock shows you when the lid is closed securely, so check it on both sides.

Don't solve the problem by tying things to the outside. Thule's instructions say no further loads may be attached to the outside of the box.

<Callout type="warning" title="Full doesn't mean within the limit">A box can be packed to the lid and still be over its weight limit if you've filled it with dense kit. Check the weight as well as the space.</Callout>

## Wet and muddy kit

Wetsuits, tents, walking boots and ski kit often come home wet. Put them in sealed or waterproof bags so water doesn't pool in the bottom of the box and soak everything else.

When you get home, empty the box, wipe it out and leave the lid open until it's dry. Thule recommends cleaning with water and ordinary washing-up liquid, and storing the box somewhere dry and out of direct sunlight.

## Keep it secure

- **Lock the box and take the key out before you drive.** Thule's instructions say to do this every time.
- **Check the locks and the load whenever you've left the car**, as Thule advises for any unattended box.
- **Keep valuables in the car or with you.** The box locks, but it's still a plastic shell on the roof.
- **Note the key number.** Thule keys are stamped with a number, usually starting with N. If you lose a key, that number makes replacing it much easier.

## Drive to the load

A loaded box makes the car taller, heavier on top and more sensitive to side winds. Thule says bars and a box can add up to 70 cm to the car's height, so watch for car parks and garages. Allow longer for braking and slow down in strong crosswinds.
