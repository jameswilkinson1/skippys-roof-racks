An electric car can make a brilliant family holiday car, and a roof box is often the only way to fit everything in. The thing you'll notice is range at motorway speed, and that's worth planning for before you set off. Here's what the published tests show, and what to check on your car before buying bars and a box.

## How much range will a roof box cost you?

More than people expect at motorway speeds, and less around town. Some of the clearest published data comes from track tests by German car magazine auto motor und sport, alongside US government figures for conventional cars:

| Source | Car | Speed | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| auto motor und sport (2022) | Kia EV6 GT | 120 km/h (75 mph) | About 14% more energy used |
| auto motor und sport (2023) | BMW iX | 100 km/h (62 mph) | 13% more energy used |
| auto motor und sport (2023) | Polestar 2 | 100 km/h (62 mph) | 26% more energy used |
| US Department of Energy, fueleconomy.gov | Petrol and diesel cars, large blunt box | 65 to 75 mph | 10% to 25% worse economy |

In range terms, a 13% to 26% rise in energy use works out at roughly 11% to 21% fewer miles from a full charge. The gap between the BMW and the Polestar shows how much the car itself matters, so treat these as a guide rather than a promise for your model.

Volkswagen's own ID.4 handbook makes the same point, warning that a fitted roof rack increases air resistance and may reduce range.

What this means in practice:

- **Plan charging stops with more margin** than usual on motorway legs.
- **Ease off a little.** US Department of Energy figures show the penalty is biggest at higher speeds.
- **Take the box off when you get home.** Thule recommends removing the box and rack when they're not in use.

## Roof load limits on EVs

Every car has a maximum roof load in its handbook, and it includes the weight of the bars and the box, not just your luggage. A heavy battery doesn't mean a stronger roof. Some EV makers also set rules about which roof racks you can use.

Here's what we could confirm from manufacturers' own published sources for popular UK EVs:

| Car | What the manufacturer says | Where it comes from |
|---|---|---|
| Volkswagen ID.4 | Maximum roof load 75 kg (165 lb), including the roof rack | VW ID.4 owner's manual |
| Skoda Enyaq | Load including Skoda's roof bars must not exceed 75 kg (for cars with roof rails, not the Coupé) | Skoda accessories catalogue |
| Tesla Model Y | Only use Tesla-approved roof rack systems, fitted with Tesla's mounting accessory | Tesla Model Y owner's manual |
| Kia EV6 | Check the figure for your version | Kia EV6 owner's handbook |

Figures can change between model years and versions, so always check the handbook for your exact car.

Now do the arithmetic. A Thule Motion 3 L weighs 20 kg and a Force 3 L weighs 18.7 kg. On a car with a 75 kg roof limit, the box alone takes a quarter of it, and the bars and feet come off that too. It's a good reason to choose a box no bigger than you need and keep heavy items in the boot.

## Glass roofs, fixpoints and rails

Lots of EVs have large glass roofs, and many have no traditional rails. That doesn't rule out a roof box, but it does mean the fitting has to be right for your exact car.

- **Fixpoints**: factory mounting points, often hidden under trim along the roof edge. Thule's Evo Fixpoint feet are rated up to 100 kg and use a car-specific fitting kit.
- **Flush rails**: rails that sit tight to the roof. Evo Flush Rail feet are rated to 75 kg and need a car-specific kit.
- **Raised rails**: rails with a gap underneath. Evo Raised Rail feet clamp straight on, with no fitting kit needed.
- **Normal roof**: no rails or fixpoints. Evo Clamp feet with a car-specific kit grip the door openings.

Fitting kits and handbook rules are specific to the exact version of your car, so tell us your car using our [which roof bars fit my car form](/which-roof-bars-fit-my-car/), including whether it has a glass roof. If you have an opening sunroof, check it still clears the bars. Thule warns that WingBar Edge sits so low that it may not be possible to open a sunroof with the racks fitted.

## Check your tailgate clearance

Many EVs are crossovers with a sloping tailgate, and a box that sits too far back will hit it when it opens. Thule lists a minimum "front clearance" for each box. This is the distance from the centre of your front roof bar to the point where the roof meets the open tailgate.

To check yours, open the tailgate fully and measure from the front bar (or roughly the top of the pillar between the front and rear doors, if you don't have bars yet) back to that point. Your measurement needs to be more than the box's figure. For example, Thule lists more than 1,304 mm (about 51 in) for the Motion 3 L and more than 1,285 mm for the Force 3 L.

<Callout type="tip" title="Steve's tip">
If your car has a powered tailgate, check the handbook to see whether you can limit how far it opens. Either way, open it slowly the first time with the box fitted and watch the gap.
</Callout>
