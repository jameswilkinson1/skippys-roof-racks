If you've ever looked up Thule roof bars for your car and been told you need "bars, feet and Kit 5xxx", this guide explains what that means. The fitting kit is the small box that does the most important job: it makes a universal set of bars and feet fit your particular roof safely.

Get the kit wrong and the bars either won't go on, or worse, they'll go on badly. Get it right and the whole system clamps on solidly and safely.

## The three parts of a Thule roof bar system

Every Thule roof bar setup is built from three parts:

- **Roof bars.** The two bars that run across the roof, such as the WingBar Evo, ProBar Evo or SquareBar Evo. These are the least car-specific part. You can see the range on our [Thule roof bars and fitting kits page](/thule-roof-bars/).
- **Foot pack.** A set of four feet that hold the bars up. The foot pack depends on your roof type: a normal roof, raised rails, flush rails or fixpoints.
- **Fitting kit.** The vehicle-specific pads, brackets or adaptors that connect the feet to your car. Each kit is designed and tested for particular vehicles.

Think of it this way: the bars carry the load, the feet suit your roof type, and the kit suits your exact car.

## Why a fitting kit is specific to your car

Roofs vary far more than most people realise. The curve of the roof, the shape of the door frame, the position of fixpoints and the profile of flush rails all change between models, and often between body styles of the same model. A hatchback and an estate version of the same car can need different kits, or a different foot pack altogether.

That's why Thule lists kits by **make, model, year and body style**. Kit 6080, for example (the one in the photo above), is a flush rail kit listed for the Toyota Corolla Touring Sports estate from 2019 and the Suzuki Swace estate from 2021. It isn't listed for the Corolla hatchback, so it wouldn't be the right kit for one.

A facelift or new generation can also change the roof, so "it's the same model" isn't enough. Always check the year range.

## Foot packs by roof type

Thule's current foot packs come in two families. **Evo** feet work with bars like the WingBar Evo, ProBar Evo and SquareBar Evo. **Edge** feet are for the low-profile WingBar Edge system, where the bar sits closer to the roof.

| Roof type | Evo foot pack | Edge foot pack | Kit needed? | Kit series |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Normal roof (no rails or fixpoints) | Evo Clamp 7105 | Edge Clamp 7205 | Yes | 5000 series |
| Flush rails (rails with no gap underneath) | Evo Flush Rail 7106 | Edge Flush Rail 7206 | Yes | 6000 series |
| Fixpoints (hidden mounting points) | Evo Fixpoint 7107 | Edge Fixpoint 7207 | Yes | 7000 series |
| Raised rails (gap under the rail) | Evo Raised Rail 7104 | Edge Raised Rail 7204 | Usually no | None |

Not sure which roof you have? Our guide to [fitting a roof box without roof rails](/guides/roof-box-without-roof-rails/) explains each roof type with what to look for.

Thule rates its Evo Clamp and Evo Flush Rail feet at 75 kg, and the Evo Fixpoint and Evo Raised Rail feet at 100 kg. Those are the feet's limits, not your car's. If your vehicle handbook gives a lower roof load, that lower figure is the one you must stick to.

## How to read a Thule kit number

Kit numbers look cryptic but follow a simple pattern. With current Evo and Edge kits, the first digit tells you the roof type:

- **Kit 5xxx**: clamp kits for normal roofs, used with the 7105 and 7205 feet
- **Kit 6xxx**: flush rail kits, used with the 7106 and 7206 feet
- **Kit 7xxx**: fixpoint kits, used with the 7107 and 7207 feet

The remaining digits identify the vehicle. You'll also see longer article numbers on boxes and websites. Kit 6080 is sold as 186080, for instance, and 5000-series clamp kits carry article numbers starting 145. It's the same kit, just Thule's catalogue reference.

<Callout type="tip" title="Steve's tip">Photograph the kit box or write the kit number in your car's handbook. If you ever need a spare part, want to sell the bars, or can't remember which feet you've got, that one number saves a lot of guesswork.</Callout>

## Older foot packs: the Rapid System

Before Evo and Edge, Thule's Rapid System feet were the standard, and plenty are still on cars or sold used. The common ones are the 754 (normal roof), 751 and 753 (fixpoints and flush rails) and 775 (raised rails).

These older feet used their own kits. The 754 clamp foot, for example, used 1000-series kits, which the current 5000-series kits replaced. The key point is that **generations don't mix**. Thule's own advice is that you can't combine old foot packs with new kits, so an Evo kit won't make a set of 754 feet fit a newer car.

If you already own a working Rapid System setup for your car, it can still be perfectly good. We supply spare parts for older and discontinued Thule products, including locks and keys, through our [Thule spare parts page](/thule-spare-parts/).

## Buying a used Thule fitting kit

Fitting kits are a good thing to buy used, as long as you're careful. Run through this list before you pay:

1. **Exact match.** Check that Thule lists that kit number for your make, model, body style and year.
2. **Right generation.** A 5000, 6000 or 7000-series kit needs Evo or Edge feet. An older kit needs the matching older feet.
3. **Complete set.** All pads and brackets (or fixpoint adaptors) should be there for all four feet. Missing one piece means the kit can't be fitted.
4. **Condition.** Rubber pads should be supple, not cracked, split or rock hard. Metal brackets should be straight, with no bends or heavy corrosion.
5. **Instructions.** The instructions show exactly where the feet go on your car. If they're missing, Thule publishes fitting instructions on its website.
