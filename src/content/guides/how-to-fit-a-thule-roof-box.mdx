Fitting a Thule roof box comes down to three things: the right bars, the right position and properly tightened clamps. With two people it's straightforward, but take your time over the positioning: it's the hardest thing to change once the clamps are tight.

These steps cover current boxes with PowerClick fittings (Motion 3, Force 3 and Pulse 2), plus the older systems you'll find on used boxes. Always follow the instructions for your exact box and bars as well.

## Before you start

You'll need:

- **Roof bars fitted correctly to your car.** If you don't have them yet, start with our [Thule roof bars and fitting kits](/thule-roof-bars/). Our guide to [fitting a roof box without roof rails](/guides/roof-box-without-roof-rails/) explains the options for different roof types.
- **A second person** for the lift.
- **The box's instructions.** Thule publishes them online if yours are missing.
- **The keys**, so you can open the box to reach the clamps.

## Step 1: Check your bars and roof load

Your car's maximum roof load is in the vehicle handbook. It covers everything on the roof, not just your luggage. Thule's instructions set out the sum: the weight of the roof bars, plus the weight of the box, plus the weight of your load, must not exceed the car's roof load limit.

The box has its own maximum load too (75 kg on current Motion, Force and Pulse boxes), and so do your bars. **Stay within all three limits.** The Highway Code (rule 98) is clear that you must not overload your vehicle and must secure your load.

Current Thule boxes weigh roughly 15 to 25 kg, depending on model and size, so check the figure for yours.

## Step 2: Set the bar spread

The bar spread is the distance between the front and rear bars. Thule's fitting data gives these limits for the current boxes:

- **Motion 3, Force 3 and Pulse 2:** roughly 55 cm minimum and 93 cm maximum.
- Where your bars' fitting instructions don't give set positions, Thule recommends **at least 70 cm** between them.

If your car has fixed mounting points, the bar spread is already decided for you. On raised roof rails you can usually slide the bars, so set them within the range above.

PowerClick clamps fit bars up to 90 mm wide. Some bar profiles need a T-track adapter instead.

## Step 3: Position the box

Get the position right before you tighten anything:

- **Front faces forward.** The pointed nose goes to the front of the car.
- **Square to the car.** Thule says the box must sit parallel to the direction of travel, not at an angle, to avoid lift at speed.
- **Centred side to side**, so it opens evenly from both sides on dual-side boxes.
- **Far enough forward to clear the tailgate.** Open the tailgate fully and check it can't touch the box.
- **Clear of the aerial.** Check the base and the opening lid don't foul a roof aerial or a roof-mounted bike carrier.

### Checking tailgate clearance

Thule publishes a "front clearance" figure for each box. Measure from the centre of your front roof bar back to the point where the roof meets the tailgate. If your measurement is **more** than the figure for the box, the open tailgate shouldn't hit it.

| Box | Thule front clearance |
|---|---|
| Pulse 2 M | 114 cm |
| Force 3 M | 115 cm |
| Pulse 2 L | 126 cm |
| Force 3 L Sport | 127 cm |
| Force 3 L | 128 cm |
| Motion 3 L | 130 cm |
| Motion 3 XL / XL Low | 132 cm |
| Force 3 XL | 135 cm |
| Pulse 2 XL | 135 cm |
| Motion 3 XXL / XXL Low | 139 cm |

These figures come from Thule's technical data, so check the instructions for your exact box too. If you're short, moving the bars forward (where your car allows it) or choosing a shorter box usually solves it.

## Step 4: Lift it on with two people

Close and lock the lid before lifting. Stand one person at each end, lift by the base rather than the lid, and lower the box gently onto the bars. A sturdy step is safer than standing in the door sills. The weight is manageable, but at up to 2.3 m long the box catches the wind and is easy to swing into the paintwork.

As Jason Thomson wrote in his Google review: "They were very helpful, showing me how to fit it and making sure I was comfortable fitting it. Even showing me the best way to lift it onto the roof bars."

## Step 5: Tighten the clamps

Open the box, slide it into its final position, and fit the clamps. How you do that depends on the mounting system.

### PowerClick (Motion 3, Force 3, Pulse 2 and older Motion XT, Force XT, Vector and Dynamic)

1. Line each of the four clamps up with the bars.
2. Hook the clamp jaws round the bar.
3. Turn the handle until you hear and feel it **click**. That's the integrated torque indicator telling you the clamp is tight enough.
4. Do all four, then go round again and check each one.

No tools are needed, and you don't need to force the handle past the click.

### FastClick (Touring and Pacific)

The clamps sit inside the box and you tighten the grip claws round the bar by turning a knob. Tighten each one firmly and evenly, following the box instructions.

### U-bolts and other older fittings

Older boxes often use U-bolts that loop under the bar and pass up through the box floor, where they're tightened from inside. Tighten them evenly so the box sits flat. Without a click to tell you when they're tight, check them more often. If you're missing any parts, we stock [Thule roof box spare parts](/thule-spare-parts/), including for discontinued models.

<Callout type="tip" title="Steve's tip">Once the box is in the right place, put a small piece of tape on each bar next to the clamps. Next time you fit it, you can drop it straight back into the same position.</Callout>

## Step 6: Lock it and do a final check

- Close the lid from both sides and make sure it's latched all the way along.
- Lock it and **take the key out**. Thule's instructions say to do this before every journey.
- Push the box firmly side to side. It should move with the car, not slide on the bars.
- Remember the extra height. Thule says bars and a box can add up to 70 cm, so watch for garages, car park barriers and drive-throughs.
- Take the box and bars off before using an automatic car wash.

## Step 7: Re-check after the first few miles

Thule recommends checking the box is firmly fixed, the load is secure and the lid is locked before and during every journey. After the first few miles, pull over somewhere safe and check every clamp and the bars, then check again at fuel or rest stops and after rough roads.

Thule's maximum speed for its boxes is 130 km/h (80 mph), above any UK speed limit. Even so, drive to the conditions and slow down in strong crosswinds. Then make sure you pack it well: our guide on [how to pack a roof box](/guides/how-to-pack-a-roof-box/) covers weight distribution and what goes where.
