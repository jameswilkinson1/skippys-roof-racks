A lost Thule key is rarely a big problem. If you can find your key number, a new key can be ordered to match, and if the number has gone too, the lock cylinders can be replaced. Whatever you do, don't reach for a screwdriver.

## What a Thule key number looks like

Thule's lock cylinders use key numbers made up of the letter N and three digits, running from **N001 to N250**. Every key and cylinder with the same number matches, whether it's on a roof box, a set of roof bar feet or a bike carrier.

That's why the number matters so much. Quote it, and a replacement key can be ordered that fits first time. Without it, there's no quick way to tell which of the 250 keys you need.

## Where to find your Thule key number

Work through these places in order:

| Where to look | What you're looking for | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Your spare key | N plus three digits, stamped on the key | Thule's manuals say the number can be found on your key |
| The face of the lock | The same code next to the keyhole | Common on older locks. Newer cylinders often leave it off for security |
| The product manual | A key number box filled in by you or the previous owner | Thule's roof box manuals include a space for it |
| Your other Thule kit | Keys for your roof bar feet or bike carrier | If you've matched your locks, it's the same number |
| Paperwork and photos | Receipts, order emails, photos of the box | Worth a look if you bought from a dealer |

If you've found it, you're nearly done. If not, skip to the section on being locked out with no number.

## Ordering a Thule replacement key

Once you have the number, ordering is straightforward:

1. **Double-check the number.** It should be N plus three digits between 001 and 250. A misread digit means the wrong key.
2. **Check the lock is a Thule cylinder.** Most Thule roof boxes, roof bar feet and bike carriers use Thule's standard cylinder. If your product has a different type of lock, send us a photo first.
3. **Order from a Thule dealer.** We supply Thule keys and lock cylinders across the N001 to N250 range, including for discontinued products. See our [Thule spare parts, keys and locks page](/thule-spare-parts/).
4. **Get two.** One to use and one to keep at home.

## Replacing the lock cylinders

Sometimes it's better to replace the cylinders rather than chase a key. Typical reasons are:

- you've lost the key and the number
- a lock has become stiff or damaged
- you bought used kit with different keys for each item
- you'd like one key for your roof box, roof bar feet and bike carrier

Thule cylinders are designed to be swapped. With the working key, you turn the cylinder to its unlocked position, then a special removal key (sometimes called a master or change key) pulls it out. The new cylinder is then fitted in its place. The removal key doesn't unlock anything. It only works on a cylinder that's already unlocked, which is what keeps the system secure. Follow the instructions supplied with the new cylinders, as the exact steps vary slightly between products.

### The Thule One-Key System

Thule's One-Key System is a set of lock cylinders that all share one key. Thule lists these pack sizes:

| Pack | Thule article number |
|---|---|
| 2 cylinders | 450200 |
| 4 cylinders | 450400 |
| 6 cylinders | 450600 |
| 8 cylinders | 450800 |
| 12 cylinders | 451200 |
| 16 cylinders | 451600 |

To work out the size you need, count every lock you want on the same key. A set of four roof bar feet needs four cylinders, then add the locks on your roof box and bike carriers.

You can't choose the key number in a One-Key pack. If you want new cylinders to match a key you already have, order individual cylinders in your existing number instead.

<Callout type="tip" title="Steve's tip">If you're replacing cylinders anyway, do the whole lot at once. One key for the box, the bar feet and the bike carrier means one number to remember and one spare to keep safe.</Callout>

## Locked out with no key and no number

This is the awkward one, but it's still solvable.

1. **Search properly first.** Check the lock face with a torch, the manual, old photos and any spare keys for other Thule kit.
2. **Talk to a Thule dealer.** Thule's own advice is that a dealer may be able to help work out the matching key number. Give us a call on 01473 785810 and we'll talk you through the options.
3. **Don't force the lid.** Prising a locked box open can crack the lid and damage the hinges and locking points, turning a key problem into a much bigger repair.
4. **Have the cylinder removed.** If the number really can't be found, the cylinder has to come out without a key, which usually means drilling it. That's a job for a locksmith or specialist, not a cordless drill on the driveway. Once it's out, new cylinders and keys can be fitted.

If the box has already been damaged trying to get in, we repair roof box locks and hinges. See our [Thule repairs page](/thule-repairs/).

## Security tips to avoid it happening again

- **Record the number now.** Write it in the manual and take a photo of the key on your phone.
- **Keep the spare at home,** not in the car's glovebox.
- **Lock and remove the key before every journey.** Thule's roof box manuals say the box must be closed and locked, and the key removed, before you drive off.
- **If the key won't come out, check the lid.** On many Thule boxes that means it isn't fully closed.
- **Look after the locks.** Thule recommends a spray lubricant for roof box locks. Keep it off the plastic of the box.
- **Don't post photos of your key online.** Keys can be ordered by number.
