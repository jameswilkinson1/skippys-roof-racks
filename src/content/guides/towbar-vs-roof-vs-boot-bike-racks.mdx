A towbar rack is the easiest to load and the best choice for e-bikes, a roof rack keeps the boot free and costs the least per bike, and a boot rack suits lighter bikes on cars with no towbar or roof bars. Three questions usually settle it: does your car have a towbar, how heavy are your bikes, and how often will you need the boot with the bikes on?

Here's how Thule's towbar, roof and boot-mounted carriers compare, with the real weight limits and the UK rules you need to know.

## The three types at a glance

**Towbar bike racks** clamp onto your car's tow ball and plug into the towbar's 13-pin socket to power their own lights. Thule's range includes the EasyFold 3, Epos, VeloSpace and VeloCompact. These are platform carriers, where the bikes stand on wheel trays and are held by frame arms.

**Roof bike racks** fit onto roof bars, one carrier per bike. The ProRide and FreeRide clamp the frame, the UpRide holds the bike by its wheels with no frame contact, and the FastRide is a fork mount, so the front wheel comes off. You'll need [Thule roof bars and the right fitting kit](/thule-roof-bars/) first.

**Boot and rear-door racks** strap or clip onto the back of the car. The OutWay (Hanging and Platform versions) attaches with steel cables and doesn't fit every car, so check Thule's fit guide. The WanderWay is a custom fit for the Volkswagen T6 van, and you'll also find the older ClipOn, made to fit specific cars, on the used market.

## Ease of loading

This is where towbar racks win. You lift each bike to around waist height, set it in the wheel trays and clamp the frame. Roof racks mean lifting the bike above head height and holding it steady while you secure it, which is hard work with a heavy bike or a tall car. Boot racks sit in between, but you're working close to the paintwork, so care is needed.

## E-bikes and weight limits

Weight is often the deciding factor. These are Thule's published limits for popular models:

- **EasyFold 3** (2-bike): 30 kg per bike, 60 kg in total. The carrier itself weighs 18.2 kg, and Thule sells an optional loading ramp.
- **Epos** (2-bike): 30 kg per bike, 60 kg in total.
- **VeloSpace XT**: 30 kg per bike.
- **VeloCompact** (2-bike): 25 kg per bike, 46 kg in total.
- **ProRide and UpRide** (roof): 20 kg per bike.
- **FastRide** (roof): 18 kg. **FreeRide** (roof): 17 kg.
- **OutWay Hanging and Platform** (boot): 15 kg per bike, 30 kg in total.
- **WanderWay** (van rear door): 30 kg per bike, 60 kg in total.

Most full-size e-bikes are heavier than a roof carrier allows, so for e-bikes it's realistically a towbar carrier.

There's a second limit to check. On a towbar, the carrier plus bikes counts towards your towbar's maximum vertical load (often called nose weight), which is in the vehicle handbook. On the roof, the bars, carriers and bikes all count towards the roof load limit. Thule's manuals are clear that the lower of the carrier's limit and the vehicle's limit is the one that applies.

## Fuel, noise and height

Bikes on the roof sit right in the airflow, so you'll notice more wind noise and higher fuel use than with bikes tucked behind the car. Roof bikes also add a lot of height. Car parks, garages, barriers and drive-throughs are the classic hazards. Towbar and boot carriers add length instead, so take care reversing and parking.

Whichever you choose, Thule's advice is to take the carrier off when you're not using it, for fuel economy and safety.

## Access to the boot

Roof racks leave the boot completely free. Many towbar carriers, including the EasyFold 3, Epos and VeloCompact, have a foot pedal that tilts the whole carrier away from the car so you can open the boot with the bikes still on. Boot-mounted racks block the boot while loaded. The OutWay lets you open the boot only once the bikes are off.

If your car has a powered tailgate, Thule's manuals say to switch off the automatic opening and open it by hand while a rear-mounted carrier is fitted, to avoid damage to the car or carrier.

## Cost

A towbar carrier is the biggest outlay, and if your car doesn't already have a towbar with 13-pin electrics, you'll need to budget for having one fitted. On Thule's UK site at the time of writing, roof carriers start at under £100 per bike, while premium towbar platforms like the Epos cost over £1,000. Roof carriers also need roof bars, which you may already own.

Check what's included. ## UK number plate and lighting rules

This is easy to get wrong. The law cares about what's visible with the bikes loaded, not just with an empty rack.

- **Number plate.** It's an offence for your registration mark to be obscured or hard to read. GOV.UK says you could be fined up to £1,000, and your car would fail its MOT, if you drive with incorrectly displayed plates. If the carrier or bikes hide the plate, fit an extra plate to the carrier. It must be made up by a registered number plate supplier, who'll need to see ID and proof you're entitled to the registration, such as your V5C.
- **Lights.** Under the Road Vehicles Lighting Regulations 1989, if a load hides any obligatory lamp or reflector, you must move it or fit an extra one where it can be seen. That applies at all times for brake lights and indicators. Thule's platform towbar carriers, such as the EasyFold 3, have lights built in for exactly this reason. With a boot-mounted rack, check that the bikes don't cover your lights, and fit a lighting board if they do.
- **Third brake light.** Thule's manuals note that on vehicles first type-approved after 1 October 1998, a rear carrier and its load mustn't cover the high-level brake light, or a replacement brake light must be fitted.
- **Keep it clean.** The Highway Code says lights, indicators, reflectors and number plates must be kept clean and clear.

<Callout type="tip" title="Steve's tip">Before every trip, load the bikes, then walk round the back of the car with someone working the brakes and indicators. If you can't read the plate or see every light clearly, sort it before you set off.</Callout>

## Which one should you choose?

- **You have a towbar, or heavy bikes or e-bikes:** a towbar carrier. It's the easiest to load and the only realistic option for e-bikes.
- **No towbar, light road or mountain bikes, and you want the boot free:** a roof carrier on Thule roof bars. See [which Thule roof bars to buy](/guides/which-thule-roof-bars/) if you're starting from scratch.
- **No towbar, no roof bars, occasional use with lighter bikes:** a boot-mounted rack such as the OutWay, if Thule lists it for your car.
- **A Volkswagen T6 van:** the WanderWay.
