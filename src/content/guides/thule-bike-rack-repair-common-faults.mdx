Most Thule bike rack faults are repairable, and many only need the right spare part and the manual. The exceptions are the structural parts that hold the carrier to the car and the bikes to the carrier, which need a specialist. Towbar carriers live a hard life, with road salt, grit, rain and the odd knock when reversing, so wear is normal.

We're the only official Thule Service Centre in the UK, and Thule's customer service team regularly sends people our way for repairs and spare parts. Here are the common faults on EasyFold, VeloSpace, VeloCompact and similar carriers, and how to tell a DIY fix from a job for the workshop.

## The one rule before you pick up a spanner

Thule's manuals say the carrier and its parts must not be modified in any way, that damaged or worn parts must be replaced straight away, and that only genuine Thule spare parts should be used.

A simple test helps. If the part holds the carrier to the car, or the bikes to the carrier, and it's bent, cracked or not locking positively, don't drive with it. Those parts take the full weight of the bikes over every pothole.

## Common faults at a glance

| Fault | Typical signs | DIY with spare parts? | Send it in if... |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tilt mechanism or foot pedal | Won't tilt, won't lock back upright, pedal bent | Cleaning only | The pedal, tilt arm or frame is bent or cracked |
| Coupling or clamp handle | Won't close, won't release, carrier moves on the ball | Adjustment per the manual | The handle or coupling body is damaged, or adjustment doesn't fix it |
| Locks and keys | Lost key, stiff or seized lock | Yes: keys and cylinders | A key has snapped inside, or the lock is damaged |
| Lights and 13-pin wiring | No lights, some lights out, flickering | Lenses, lamps, simple checks | The cable or plug is damaged, or the fault is inside the wiring |
| Wheel straps and frame holders | Split straps, worn ratchets, missing pads | Yes | The frame holder arm or its mounting is bent |
| Folding hinge | Play, rattles, hinge won't lock open | No | Always |

## Tilt mechanism and foot pedal

Most of Thule's platform carriers, including the EasyFold 3, Epos and VeloCompact, tilt away from the car when you press a foot pedal, so you can open the boot with the bikes on. Faults usually show up as a pedal that won't release, a carrier that won't tilt, or one that won't click back upright.

Dirt and road salt in the mechanism can stop it working smoothly. Clean it with warm water or car shampoo, as Thule's manuals recommend, and let it dry. If that doesn't free it, or the pedal or tilt arm is bent from a knock, stop there. The tilt mechanism holds the loaded carrier in place, so a bent or cracked part needs replacing by someone who knows the carrier.

## Coupling handle won't lock or won't release

The coupling is the clamp that grips the tow ball. Most Thule towbar carriers have an adjustment knob or wheel that sets how tightly it grips, and some models have an indicator that shows when it's secure. If the handle is too stiff to close, or the carrier moves on the ball once it's closed, the adjustment is usually the first thing to check. Your manual explains how.

Also check that the tow ball is clean and undamaged. Thule's manuals say to lubricate the coupling mechanism, but not the surfaces that touch the tow ball.

Don't use an extension bar or force on the handle, and don't hammer a coupling that won't let go. If adjustment doesn't solve it, or the handle or coupling body is cracked or bent, send it in. This is the part that holds everything to your car.

## Stuck or broken locks and lost keys

Lost keys are the easy one. Thule keys have a number, an N followed by three digits, so a replacement can be ordered to match. A stiff lock may free up with a little spray lubricant. If you've lost the number, or want one key for everything, the lock cylinders can be swapped. Our guide to [lost Thule keys](/guides/lost-thule-roof-box-key/) covers all of this, and we supply keys and cylinders through our [Thule spare parts page](/thule-spare-parts/).

A key snapped inside the lock, or a lock that's been forced, is a workshop job.

## Lights and 13-pin wiring

Towbar carriers with integrated lights take power through a 13-pin plug. When lights fail, work from the car outwards:

- **Check the car first.** Test the towbar socket with another trailer or a tester if you can. If nothing works at all, the fault may be in the car's towbar electrics or a fuse, not the carrier.
- **Check the plug.** Make sure it's pushed fully home and twisted to lock. Look for corroded or bent pins.
- **Check the lamps.** A cracked lens or failed lamp, often the result of a knock, is usually simple to replace with the right spare.
- **Check the cable.** Look along its length for cuts, crushing or chafing.

If the plug, cable or internal wiring is damaged, it's best sent in. Remember that a carrier without working lights isn't legal to use on the road if it hides the car's own lights.

## Broken wheel straps and frame holders

Rubber wheel straps wear and perish over time. Thule's manuals say to check them for wear and replace them if worn. Straps and many frame-holder parts are straightforward to replace with genuine spares.

When you order, give the carrier's model and serial number. Thule's manuals recommend this, and it makes sure you get the right part first time. If the frame holder arm itself is bent, or the part it bolts to is damaged, send it in.

## Folding hinge wear

Folding carriers like the EasyFold and VeloCompact are brilliant for storage, but every fold puts wear on the hinges. Signs include play or rattles in the frame, a hinge that won't lock fully open, or visible cracks. The hinges carry the bikes' weight, so this is never a DIY job: don't use the carrier until it's been checked. A failure at speed puts other road users at risk, not just your bikes.

## Keeping your carrier working

A little care goes a long way:

- Clean the carrier with warm water or car shampoo, especially after winter roads or coastal trips.
- Store it somewhere dry when it's off the car.
- Take it off before using an automatic car wash.
- Check bolts and knobs are tight now and then, and, as Thule advises, check the carrier is still secure after the first 30 miles (50 km) of every trip.
- Check the straps every season.

<Callout type="tip" title="Steve's tip">Give the carrier a rinse with warm water after any winter trip, before you put it away. Salt left in the tilt and coupling mechanisms can make them stiff by the time you next need them.</Callout>
