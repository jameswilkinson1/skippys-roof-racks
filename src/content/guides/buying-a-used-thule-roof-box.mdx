A used Thule box can save you a lot of money, but only if it's complete and everything works. Most problems with second-hand boxes are easy to spot in ten minutes, as long as you know where to look.

Work through the checklist below, ideally with the box on a table or the ground so you can see underneath.

## First, know which box you're looking at

The model name is usually on the lid, and there's a label inside with the details. Thule also puts a **BA number** sticker inside the lid, which identifies the exact version when you order spare parts. Here's what to expect from the Thule boxes you're most likely to come across used:

## The 10-point checklist

### 1. Lid alignment

Close the lid and look along the seam on both sides. The gap should be even from front to back, with the lid sitting square on the base. A lid that sits high at one corner, or needs pressing down to latch, may have been twisted or overloaded. It may not lock reliably or keep the rain out.

### 2. Hinges and dual-side opening

Most of these boxes open from both sides. Open the lid fully from one side, close it, then do the same from the other. Each side should open smoothly and latch shut without force. Stiffness, grinding or a corner that won't latch points to worn or broken hinge parts. They can be repaired, but factor the repair into the price.

### 3. Lock and keys

Lock and unlock the box from each side and make sure the lid really is held shut when locked. Ask how many keys come with it: a new Thule box normally comes with two.

Thule keys have a number stamped on them, usually an **N-number** such as N123. Note it down. On some older locks the number is also on the face of the cylinder, but newer cylinders leave it off for security. If the box has no keys, or only one, it isn't a deal-breaker: replacement keys and lock cylinders are available. Our guide to [what to do if you've lost your Thule roof box key](/guides/lost-thule-roof-box-key/) explains how.

### 4. Mounting hardware: complete, and which system

This is the big one. A box without its clamps can't go on the car. Count them: a PowerClick box has four, like the Force 3 in the photo above. Then check which system it uses:

- **PowerClick** (Motion 3, Force 3, Pulse 2, Motion XT, Force XT, Vector, Dynamic): each clamp has a handle you turn until it clicks.
- **FastClick** (Touring and Pacific): you tighten the grip claws round the bar by turning knobs.
- **U-bolts** on older boxes: these loop under the bar and pass up through the floor of the box, where they're tightened from inside.

Check each clamp for cracks and stripped threads, and on PowerClick clamps make sure the handle still clicks. If anything is missing, we stock [Thule roof box spare parts](/thule-spare-parts/), including parts for discontinued models.

### 5. Lid lifters

Open the lid fully and let go. It should stay up on its own. If it sinks, or drops when you nudge it, the lid lifters are tired. They're replaceable, but a lid that won't stay open is a pain when you're loading in the wind.

### 6. Cracks and UV fading

Turn the box over or look underneath. Check around the clamp mounting points, the lock and the hinges, and along the front where stone chips land. White stress lines in the plastic are an early sign of cracking.

Faded or chalky plastic is usually just sun damage from being stored outside. Thule advises storing boxes out of the rain and direct sunlight. Press on faded areas: the plastic should flex slightly, not feel brittle.

<Callout type="warning" title="Walk away from structural cracks">A crack near a clamp mounting point, a hinge or the lock isn't cosmetic. That's where the box takes the load at speed, so don't buy a box with damage there, however cheap it is.</Callout>

### 7. Internal straps

Many Thule boxes, including the Motion XT, Force XT and Force 3, come with internal tie-down straps that clip to anchor points inside. They stop your load sliding about under braking. Replacement straps are available, but it's worth knowing before you pay.

### 8. Instructions

Instructions are nice to have but not essential. Thule publishes them as downloads on its website, and the BA number sticker inside the lid tells you exactly which version you have. Check the sticker is still there and readable.

### 9. Max load label

Look for the label inside the box stating the maximum load. Current Motion, Force and Pulse boxes are rated at 75 kg, but some older ranges are lower: the Touring, for example, is rated at 50 kg. Remember your car's roof load limit, in the vehicle handbook, includes the weight of the bars and the box itself. The lowest limit always wins.

### 10. Test fit on your bars

Finally, make sure the box suits your bars and your car. PowerClick clamps fit bars up to 90 mm wide, and the current Motion 3, Force 3 and Pulse 2 accept a bar spacing of roughly 55 to 93 cm. Check the tailgate can open fully with the box in place, too. If you don't yet have bars, our [which roof bars fit my car](/which-roof-bars-fit-my-car/) page will point you to the right ones.

<Callout type="tip" title="Steve's tip">If you can, take the car with you when you collect a used box and fit it there and then. Ten minutes on the car tells you more than any photo.</Callout>
