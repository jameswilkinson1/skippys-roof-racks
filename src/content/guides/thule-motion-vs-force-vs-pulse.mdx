The three ranges share most of their engineering, so the choice usually comes down to looks, size options and budget rather than safety or security. As the only official Thule Service Centre in the UK, we see every generation of these boxes come through the workshop, and below is how they really compare.

The current line-up is the **Motion 3** (which replaced the Motion XT), the **Force 3** (which replaced the Force XT) and the **Pulse 2**, a new generation of Thule's lower-priced Pulse box.

The headline is that all three carry the same 75 kg, all open from either side of the car, and all now use Thule's PowerClick mounting. Choosing the Pulse 2 doesn't mean a lower load rating or a different fitting system.

## Sizes compared

Similar size names don't always mean identical boxes. The Motion 3 is 10 litres smaller at M, L and XL, while the Force 3 and Pulse 2 match each other.

A few sizes are worth knowing about:

- **Force 3 S** is only 140 cm long. It suits small cars and short roofs where a longer box would hang over the windscreen or tailgate.
- **Force 3 L Sport and XXL Sport** are narrow (63 cm and 70 cm wide), leaving bar space for a bike or kayak. The XXL Sport takes skis up to 215 cm.
- **Motion 3 Low** versions trade space for height. The XL Low is about 36 cm tall against about 43 cm for the standard XL.
- **Motion 3 XXL** at 590 litres is the biggest of the current three, and takes skis up to 215 cm.

## Opening, lid and lock

All three have dual-side opening, so you can load from the pavement side whichever way you're parked.

**Motion 3** has a wide handle designed for opening and closing with one hand. Its **SlideLock** keeps locking and opening as separate actions, locks the lid automatically when you shut it and shows you when it's closed properly.

**Force 3** uses the same SlideLock system with an integrated handle. Both the Motion 3 and Force 3 hold the lid up with lid lifters at the hinges, and spare lifters are available if one weakens over the years.

**Pulse 2** has a central lock that confirms when the lid is closed. It's a simpler set-up, but it still opens from both sides.

On the Motion 3 and Force 3, Thule sells a lid pull strap (handy on tall cars) and a box light as optional extras. Neither comes in the box.

<Callout type="tip" title="Steve's tip">Whichever box you buy, write down the N-number stamped on the key as soon as you get it home. Some newer lock cylinders don't show the number, and the key is the easiest place to find it.</Callout>

## Mounting: PowerClick on all three

Older and cheaper Thule boxes used a mix of fittings, but the Motion 3, Force 3 and Pulse 2 all use **PowerClick**. Each of the four clamps hooks round the roof bar, and you turn the handle until it clicks. The click is the torque indicator telling you the clamp is tight enough.

According to Thule's fitting data, all three accept a roof bar spacing of roughly 55 to 93 cm. PowerClick clamps fit bars up to 90 mm wide, and some bars need a T-track adapter instead. For example, Thule lists an adapter for the Pulse 2 on ProBar Evo bars.

## Positioning and boot access

Thule describes all three as sitting forward on the roof so the tailgate can open fully with minimal risk of hitting the box. In practice that still depends on your car, bar position and box length. Longer boxes need more room between the front bar and the top of the tailgate, so a hatchback with a steep tailgate may suit an M or L better than an XL.

## Which one suits you?

- **Choose the Motion 3** if you want the sleekest look, the widest size range (including XXL and the low-profile versions), or a gloss finish to match the car.
- **Choose the Force 3** if you want a tough, textured matt finish, or a specialist shape: the short S for small cars, or the narrow Sport boxes alongside a bike carrier.
- **Choose the Pulse 2** if you want a proper Thule box with PowerClick, dual-side opening and 75 kg capacity at the lowest price, and an M, L or XL covers your needs.

## Thule Pulse 2: what you get and what you give up

If you've searched for a Pulse 2 review, here are the facts. You get the same 75 kg load rating, PowerClick mounting, dual-side opening and forward positioning as the dearer boxes. The Pulse 2 is also light for its size: the L weighs 18.7 kg, the same as a Force 3 L.

What you give up is SlideLock (it has a central lock instead), the Sport, short and low-profile sizes, and some of the styling. For a family doing a few trips a year, that's often a sensible trade.

## Motion XT vs Force XT: buying the older generation used

The Motion XT (launched in 2016) and Force XT (2018) are the previous-generation boxes you're most likely to find used. Both carry 75 kg, open from both sides and use PowerClick mounting with a torque indicator, so they work in much the same way as the current boxes.

| | Motion XT | Force XT |
|---|---|---|
| Finish | Gloss | Matt AeroSkin |
| Lock | SlideLock | Central lock |
| Sizes | Sport 300 L, M 400 L, L 450 L, XL 500 L, XXL 610 L, Alpine 450 L | Sport 300 L, S 300 L, M 400 L, L 450 L, XL 500 L, Alpine 420 L |
| Max load | 75 kg | 75 kg |

The Motion XT XXL is worth noting at 610 litres, which is bigger than today's Motion 3 XXL. The Alpine versions are long boxes designed around skis.

A well-kept XT is a good buy, but check it carefully: lid alignment, both sets of hinges, the lock and keys, and that all four clamps are there.
